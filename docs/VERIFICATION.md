# Verification record

The analyses in this repository have been through four independent verification passes. This file summarizes them; the underlying working logs are retained in the authors' archive and available on reasonable request.

**1 — Reproduction audit (2026-06-21, repeated 2026-07-02).** The entire pipeline was re-run from a pristine copy of the dataset in an isolated directory, following the reproduction instructions exactly. Every headline number regenerated identically: the inter-coder reliability table (32 of 53 variables at κ ≥ 0.70), the masters-roster counts (R1 42 named exemplars, 31.0 % female; R2 61, 19.7 % female), the LLM-jury statistics (Kendall W = 0.856; top-rated stories S_06 and S_02), the human-jury results (S_07 6.50 > S_06 4.57 > S_04 4.50 > S_02 4.40; the descriptive rank inversion relative to the LLM jury on the four shared stories), and all figures. Derived files were byte-identical apart from ~1e-16 floating-point noise in κ values.

**2 — Claim-by-claim check (2026-07-02).** Every empirical claim in the submitted abstract was traced to its supporting data and matched.

**3 — Ground-truth coding audit (2026-08-20).** Independent blind re-coders — **LLM agents**, given only the source texts and the rubric's verbatim definitions, with no access to the recorded codes or to each other — re-coded all 48 personas (8 variables) and all 8 stories (13 variables); the masters roster was independently re-extracted from the six critic reports; and both jury data chains were re-checked from raw responses through to the reported statistics. Being machine re-coders, these passes verify the *textual fidelity* of the recorded codes against the corpus; the construct validity of the rubric's categories is a matter of interpretation, addressed in the paper and its supplement. Outcomes:

- Across 384 persona checks, the blind re-codes reproduced the paper's headline persona results (gender, multilingualism, outsider relation, Nobel year) exactly from the texts. Where the two original coders disagreed, the re-coder's value fell within their range; that criterion is weak by construction and should not be read as adjudicating the disagreement. The `region` distribution was reproduced in shape, but this variable falls below the reliability threshold and is reported descriptively (see below).
- Across 104 story checks, the only divergences fell on the two most interpretive variables, at rates consistent with the reported reliability.
- The independent masters re-extraction confirmed the reported gender composition under every defensible inclusion variant (R1 67.5–71.1 % male; R2 79.4–80.6 %), and confirmed the absence of MENA exemplars in all reports.
- Both jury chains reproduced with zero discrepancies (Level 2: all 60 score rows for the four Level-3 stories — five jurors × three repetitions each — against raw juror responses, plus weighting, rankings, self-preference, and vendor blinding, which held with the documented exception of persona names retained inside story text; Level 3: all 140 response values, weighted means, detection counts, rank correlation, and bootstrap confidence intervals).

**4 — Pre-release integrity audit (2026-08-20).** A structural sweep of the shipped repository checked every cross-reference, the completeness of the corpus index, the anonymized human-jury export, and all 120 LLM-jury response blocks for duplication. It found one duplicated juror response, which was recovered from the original vendor session and restored; see `DATA_REPAIR_2026-08-20.md` for the defect, the recovery, and its (small, single-story) effect on results.

Known, documented limitations of the dataset are discussed in the paper and its supplement: the below-threshold reliability of the `region` variable and of six of the thirteen story-level variables (all reported descriptively); the vendor-personalization confound; the structural asymmetry between the scaffolded HIGH prompt and the single-sentence controls, which makes the control comparison a contrast of prompt regimes rather than an isolation of prestige; and minor inclusion-boundary judgments in the masters roster.
