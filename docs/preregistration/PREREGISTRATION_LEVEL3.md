# Pre-registration — Level 3 (human-expert jury evaluation of LLM-jury top-3 stories)

*Locked: May 24, 2026. Drafted by Michaela Liegertová with Claude as methodological partner. Sent for external AI peer consultation (GPT + Gemini) before any human evaluator is invited. This document succeeds and complements `PREREGISTRATION_v1.md`, `PREREGISTRATION_v1_ADDENDUM.md`, and `PREREGISTRATION_LEVEL2.md`.*

## Aim

Obtain a designed, independent human-expert evaluation of the three stories the LLM jury (Level 2) rated highest, so that the paper has a primary human-anchored comparator for the LLM-jury verdict.

The Level 3 sub-study replaces the SOLIT colloquium feedback (n=3, opportunistic, qualitative) as the paper's primary human-aesthetic anchor. SOLIT data are retained in the paper as **directional / pilot context**, not as the human-jury comparator.

## Stories evaluated

The three highest-rated stories from the LLM jury (Level 2, May 24, 2026):

1. **S_06** — *The Drawer for Returned Names* (Nadira Kovač persona, generated by GPT-5.5, Run 2 / 2026). LLM-jury grand mean = 8.66 (cross-LLM SD = 0.45).
2. **S_02** — *Birthälm, in November* (Magda Aron persona, generated by Claude Opus 4.7, Run 2 / 2026). LLM-jury grand mean = 8.38 (cross-LLM SD = 0.39).
3. **S_07** — *The Translator's Hands* (Elara Kostova persona, generated by Claude Opus 4, Run 1 / 2025). LLM-jury grand mean = 6.97 (cross-LLM SD = 1.40). This story was also evaluated by the n=3 SOLIT pilot panel (human mean 7.67).

These three constitute a non-overlapping sample across:
- 2 R2 stories + 1 R1 story (cross-vintage)
- 2 different generating model families (Claude / Opus, GPT)
- Story-archetype variation (S_06 archive-translator; S_02 Saxon-village return; S_07 mother-daughter death-in-language)

## Human panel composition

- **Target n = 8–10 evaluators**, **minimum n = 5** (matching the LLM-jury panel size).
- Mixed expertise:
  - 2–3 literary scholars / critics (≥ 1 contemporary fiction focus)
  - 1–2 creative writers (published prose fiction or essays)
  - 1 translator (cross-cultural literary expression)
  - 1 literary editor / magazine editor
  - 1 comparative-literature scholar (preferred: cross-tradition)
- **Linguistic mix**: Czech + anglophone, reflecting the project's existing network (SOLIT contacts + UJEP comp-lit + international invitations).
- **Inclusion of SOLIT reviewers (n=3)**: yes. The three SOLIT human reviewers (literary critic, creative writer, literary critic-Czech) are invited to participate in Level 3 because (a) all three explicitly agreed to future collaboration on the SOLIT form, and (b) their re-rating of S_07 enables a within-rater longitudinal stability check across ~8 months. Their Level 3 evaluations are reported separately from new evaluators where useful.
- **Conflicts of interest**: no evaluator is informed that their participation is intended to validate or invalidate any specific aesthetic claim. Evaluators are told what the study is about (see "Disclosure to evaluators" below).

## Procedure

1. **Invitation**: personal email from the lead author (Michaela Liegertová), with brief project description, time estimate (~45 minutes per story), and link to the evaluation form.
2. **Evaluation form**: a Microsoft / Google Form, an updated version of the SIMPLEX form (`SIMPLEX Literary Evaluation Form`, used at SOLIT), with the rubric structure adapted to match the Level 2 LLM-jury rubric for direct comparability.
3. **Story presentation**: all three stories in a single form, randomized order per respondent. Story IDs blinded to source (presented as Story A, Story B, Story C with stable codes mapping back to S_06, S_02, S_07 — mapping held privately).
4. **Disclosure to evaluators**: evaluators are told that the stories are **AI-generated outputs from a research workflow studying LLM-mediated literary creation**. This matches the SOLIT disclosure precedent. The choice is for methodological honesty over fully blinded conditions; the study is not a Turing-style detection experiment.
5. **No deliberation phase**: each evaluator scores independently. No panel discussion. Disagreement is part of the data.
6. **Stopping rule**: target completion by **June 22, 2026**. Final close: **June 28, 2026** (one-week buffer before July 1 abstract deadline). Minimum n=5; if fewer respond by close, an extension addendum is filed and the analysis runs at whatever n is available with appropriate caveats.

## Evaluation instrument

The evaluation form contains:

### Numerical section (matches Level 2 LLM-jury rubric)

For each of the three stories, scores 1–10 (integer) on the same five criteria the LLM jury used:

- **Literary Merit** (language mastery, style, voice, technical execution)
- **Originality** (fresh perspectives, innovative approaches, unique narrative choices)
- **Emotional Impact** (ability to move, provoke, or transform the reader)
- **Thematic Depth** (complexity of ideas, universal resonance, intellectual engagement)
- **Structural Craft** (pacing, plot construction, narrative architecture)

Scale anchors identical to the LLM-jury rubric (10 = once-in-a-decade; 8–9 = strong / award-quality; 6–7 = competent / publishable; 4–5 = flawed; 1–3 = below threshold).

### Editorial decision

For each story: "If you were editing a literary journal and this was a submission, would you: (Accept / Accept with revisions / Reject / Cannot judge)."

### Qualitative section (preserved from the SOLIT form)

- Would you have suspected non-human authorship? (Yes / Probably yes / Uncertain / Probably no / No)
- Free-text: what elements feel "AI-like"
- Free-text: what elements feel genuinely creative or surprising
- Strongest aspect of these stories (closed-list + other)
- Weakest aspect (closed-list + other)
- Free-text on the methodology itself

### Background metadata (optional)

- Background category (literary critic / creative writer / translator / editor / comp-lit scholar / other)
- Primary working language
- Years of experience in literary criticism, editing, or creative writing

## Pre-specified analyses

**Primary outcome: human-jury verdict on the top-3.**

- Per-story human grand mean (weighted total across all responding evaluators) with 95% CI.
- Per-story editorial-decision distribution (% accept / accept-with-revisions / reject).
- Human ranking of the 3 stories.

**Secondary outcomes (pre-specified):**

| Q | Analysis |
|---|---|
| L3-1 | **LLM-jury vs human-jury ranking agreement on top-3**: Kendall's τ_b between the LLM mean ranking and the human mean ranking on these 3 stories |
| L3-2 | **Per-story mean comparison**: human mean − LLM mean per story; report with 95% CI from the human side (LLM cell-mean is fixed) |
| L3-3 | **Distribution-shape comparison on S_07**: human n=3 SOLIT + new human evaluators + LLM-jury 15 cells, visualized as parallel distributions. Visual emphasis on whether the shape of disagreement matches |
| L3-4 | **Per-rater alignment**: for each LLM juror, compute Spearman ρ between its 3-story rank vector and each human evaluator's 3-story rank vector. Identify LLM-human "type" mappings |
| L3-5 | **Within-rater longitudinal stability** (SOLIT reviewers only, on S_07 only): the 3 SOLIT reviewers' Level 3 score on S_07 vs their original SOLIT score (Sep 2025). Bland-Altman-style plot for the 3 paired observations; pure descriptive |
| L3-6 | **Per-criterion convergence**: per-criterion human grand mean vs LLM grand mean per story; identify which criteria humans and LLMs converge/diverge on |
| L3-7 | **Editorial decision concordance**: % of evaluators who would accept-or-accept-with-revisions per story; compared (qualitatively) to LLM-jury's implicit acceptance (LLM mean weighted ≥ 7) |
| L3-8 | **AI-detection rate**: % of human evaluators who would have suspected non-human authorship; compared to the n=3 SOLIT pilot rate (1 yes / 1 uncertain / 1 no) |

## What this study does NOT pre-register

- Any claim about whether AI-generated literary fiction "passes" or "fails" human-expert review. Editorial-decision rates are reported descriptively; no threshold is set in advance for "passing."
- Any claim about generalization beyond the 3 stories evaluated.
- Any claim about generalization beyond the specific human panel recruited. Panel composition is not random sampling from a defined population.
- Any claim that human-jury verdict = literary truth. The human panel is a *second* aesthetic-judgment instrument, not an oracle.

## Acknowledged limitations (pre-specified)

1. **Sample size**: target n=8–10, minimum n=5. Statistical power for direct mean comparisons with the n=15 LLM cells per story is limited. Effect sizes and CIs are the primary reporting, not p-values.
2. **Self-selection**: evaluators who agree to participate may differ from those who decline (e.g., more receptive to AI-in-literature research).
3. **Disclosed AI condition**: evaluators know the stories are AI-generated; this may affect scoring (in either direction — more lenient as "interesting research outputs," or stricter as "should clear a higher bar"). This is methodological honesty over experimental control.
4. **Linguistic mix**: Czech + anglophone evaluators introduce reading-language variance; we do not control for this.
5. **Non-blinded comparison panel**: the LLM jury panel was metadata-blinded by anonymization; the human panel is disclosed-AI. Direct LLM-vs-human comparisons should acknowledge this asymmetry.
6. **SOLIT pilot data is not designed**: the n=3 SOLIT data is a colloquium feedback exercise. The paper treats it as **directional pilot evidence**, not as a designed evaluation. Reported in the paper as supporting context; Level 3 is the primary human anchor.
7. **Within-rater longitudinal check (SOLIT reviewers' S_07 re-rating)** has n=3, providing only descriptive observations about score stability across ~8 months.
