# corpus/ — the texts

Everything the models wrote, verbatim, organized by run:

- `prompts/` — the commissioning prompts for the three phases (critic → biographer → writer) and the two control conditions. The story phase (`phase3_short_story.md`) includes its recorded follow-up turn — a brief "do you want to do more work on it?" prompt that was part of the deployed elicitation.
- `run1_2025/` and `run2_2026/` — the two main runs (July 2025, May 2026): `reports/` (the models as critics), `personas/` (the imagined novelists' biographies), `stories/` (their fiction). Run 1 was a smaller baseline (one persona and one story per model); Run 2 is the primary sample (five personas per model, one story from a selected persona). Each run has three critic reports, not four: Grok offered no deep-research mode in either run (as of August 2026 it still does not), so all Phase 2 persona generation — Grok's included — worked from the three available reports.
- `controls_2026/` — control personas from single-sentence prompts, quoted exactly: `random/` (NEUTRAL condition, "Create a random writer persona.") and `average/` (LOW condition, "Create an average writer persona."). The main runs are the HIGH condition (the full three-phase scaffold). These are the condition codes used in `index.csv` and throughout the analysis files.
- `index.csv` — one row per text, nine fields: file path, run, condition, type, model code and full model name, replicate number, persona name, story title.
- `share_urls.md` — vendor share links for the generations where they exist, with the provenance caveats stated in that file.

**All personas are invented figures.** Their biographies, bibliographies, and prizes are fictitious, generated by the models under the study's prompts; any resemblance to real persons is the phenomenon under study, not biography.

**These texts also make false statements about real people and real institutions.** Beyond inventing their subjects, the generated biographies attribute specific fabricated acts to real, identifiable third parties: reviews and quoted verdicts placed in the mouths of named critics (among them Susan Sontag, Zadie Smith, James Wood, Hans Magnus Enzensberger, and Anders Olsson of the Swedish Academy); invented interviews and translations credited to real journalists and translators; invented coverage credited to real periodicals (*Die Zeit*, *The New Yorker*, *The New York Review of Books*, *The Paris Review*, and others); real literary prizes recorded as won by fictional writers, in prize-years whose real laureates are living people; and fabricated archival provenance, with real repositories described as holding a fictional writer's papers. Every such statement is model output generated under this study's prompts and is **false**. None of it may be quoted, cited, indexed, or reproduced as fact about any real person or organisation — several files present these claims without any fictional framing of their own, so this notice is the framing. These files are research data documenting how language models fabricate; they are not a source of biographical, bibliographic, or critical information.

**What is verbatim.** Every model-output file opens with a one-line archival label, `# <filename>`, identical to the file's name — added at packaging time, normalized to this single convention in the public tree (collection-time labels varied in format; the authors' archive preserves the originals). Everything below the label is model output, untouched — typos, formatting inconsistencies, and trailing conversational offers included (one Run 1 story retains the model's own preamble). The six reference files here — the five prompts in `prompts/` and `share_urls.md` — are documentation, not model output, and carry ordinary headings.
