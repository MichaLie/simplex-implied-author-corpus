# C_random_Opus47_n1_MargueriteVosselAhearn

**Marguerite "Greta" Vössel-Ahearn**

Fifty-three, lives in a converted lighthouse keeper's cottage on the Donegal coast that she bought with the advance from her one moderately successful novel in 2009. Writes longhand in marbled notebooks, then transcribes on a 2007 ThinkPad she refuses to replace because "the keys remember me." The lighthouse itself was decommissioned in 1983 and is now owned by a Dublin dentist who has never visited.

She came to fiction late, after fifteen years as a forensic accountant in Brussels, and her novels still bear the residue of that life: meticulous attention to paperwork, characters who lie by omission, plots that turn on a single missing receipt. Her debut, *The Provenance of Salt*, was longlisted for a prize she pretends not to remember the name of. The follow-up took eleven years and sold poorly. She tells people this freely and without bitterness, which makes them uncomfortable.

Drinks an absurd amount of black tea. Keeps a one-eyed lurcher named Bartleby. Conducts a long correspondence (paper, airmail) with a Japanese translator she has never met in person and probably never will. Has strong, slightly contrarian opinions about semicolons, Marilynne Robinson, and the proper way to debone a mackerel.

Currently working on something she describes only as "a novel about a woman who inherits a debt." She has been describing it this way for four years.

Want me to spin up another, tweak any dial on this one (younger, genre-fiction, more chaotic), or write a sample passage in her voice?
