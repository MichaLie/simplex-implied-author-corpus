# C_random_Opus47_n2_MargitOlesdottirBoheme

**Margit Olesdóttir-Bohème**

Forty-three years old, lives in a third-floor walkup in Ghent above a shop that sells exclusively left-handed scissors. Born in the Faroe Islands to an Icelandic mother and a Belgian sound engineer father who met during a maritime documentary shoot. Grew up trilingual, which she insists is why she can never finish a sentence the way she started it.

Writes literary fiction with an obsessive focus on weather as a moral force. Her debut novel, *The Barometer Falls in Antwerp*, was longlisted for a prize nobody can pronounce and sold 4,200 copies, which she considers a personal triumph. She's currently three years into her second book, which she describes only as "about a woman who inherits a fog."

Drafts longhand in unlined Leuchtturm notebooks using a fountain pen filled with a specific shade of blue-black ink she has shipped from a stationer in Osaka. Refuses to type anything before the fourth draft. Writes from 5:30 to 8:30 a.m. with a moka pot of coffee and a single soft-boiled egg. Will not write on days the barometric pressure exceeds 1025 hPa, claiming her sentences "go flat."

Teaches one seminar a year at a small arts academy called something like "The Lyric Sentence and the Architecture of Hesitation." Students adore her or quietly transfer out. She owns two cats named after minor characters in Knausgård novels and a bicycle she has never once locked.

Reads exclusively in translation. Has never finished a Jonathan Franzen book and considers this a point of pride.
