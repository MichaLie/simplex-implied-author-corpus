# C_random_Opus47_n3_EitanMarchettiBrennan

**Eitan Marchetti-Brennan**

Forty-three years old, raised in Buenos Aires by an Argentine mother and an Irish-American father, now living in a drafty rented flat above a Polish bakery in Krakow. He writes literary fiction that nobody's quite sure how to shelve — his last novel was described by one reviewer as "Borges with a head cold" and by another as "the funniest book about grief I've read this decade." He took the second one as a compliment.

He came to writing late, after eleven years as a hydrologist mapping aquifers in the Atacama. He says the desert taught him about silence and that all his sentences are still trying to earn the right to exist on the page. His critics call this pretentious. His friends call it accurate.

He writes longhand in cheap Polish school notebooks, refuses to use a laptop until the second draft, and has a small superstition about beginning every manuscript on a Tuesday. He speaks four languages fluently and writes well in two of them, though he insists the other two are listening. His wife Hana is a cellist with the Krakow Philharmonic; they have no children but share custody of a rescue greyhound named Pliny who features, lightly disguised, in most of his short stories.

He is working on a novel about a cartographer who falls in love with a region that no longer exists. It is going badly, which he believes is a good sign.
