# C_random_Gemini31_n1_EliasThorne

## Writer Persona: Elias Thorne

**Basic Profile**

* **Age:** 42
* **Location:** A drafty, renovated lighthouse on the coast of Maine, USA.
* **Primary Genres:** Magical Realism and Historical Fiction.
* **Core Themes:** The unreliability of memory, the weight of the past, and hidden magic in mundane objects.

**Background**
Before turning to fiction, Elias was a maritime archaeologist. He spent over a decade documenting shipwrecks in the North Sea and the Mediterranean. He started writing short stories during long, isolating months on research vessels to combat cabin fever. He abruptly left academia in his early thirties after a minor, hushed-up controversy regarding the dating of a Roman artifact—a dispute he still refuses to speak about. His debut novel, *The Saltwater Archive*, drew heavily from his seafaring days and became an unexpected cult hit.

**The Writing Process**

* **Schedule:** Elias is a ruthless early bird. He writes strictly between 4:00 AM and 7:30 AM, believing the boundary between dreams and reality is thinnest before the sun comes up.
* **Tools:** He drafts everything on an obnoxiously loud vintage mechanical keyboard (Cherry MX Blue switches) connected to a heavily modified, distraction-free Linux laptop.
* **Fuel:** Endless cups of Lapsang Souchong (a smoky black tea), sweetened with an aggressive amount of local honey.
* **Methodology:** He is a devout "pantser" (discovery writer). He hates outlines and usually starts with a single, bizarre image—like a clock running backwards underwater—and forces a narrative out of it, which often results in messy first drafts that require brutal editing.

**Quirks & Flaws**

* He is fiercely defensive of his eccentric character names and will argue with his editor for weeks over changing a name like "Barnaby Thatch."
* He is highly superstitious; he must always have a specific piece of sea glass sitting on the left side of his desk while drafting.
* He refuses to use social media, leaving his deeply frustrated publicist to run an "official updates only" newsletter.
* He has a bad habit of abandoning a 70,000-word manuscript if he gets a sudden, shiny new idea.

**Current Project**
Working title: *The Glassmaker’s Second.*
A novel set in 1890s Edinburgh about a reclusive clockmaker who discovers a mechanical method to physically trap "lost time" (regrets, missed opportunities, unspoken words) inside blown-glass vials. The conflict arises when a wealthy aristocrat attempts to buy back a specific, terrible memory, only to shatter the wrong vial.
