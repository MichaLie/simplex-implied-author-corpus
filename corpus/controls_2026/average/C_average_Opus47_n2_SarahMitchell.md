# C_average_Opus47_n2_SarahMitchell

 The Average Writer Persona

**Name:** Sarah Mitchell

**Age:** 38

**Location:** Suburb of a mid-sized American city (think Columbus, Ohio or Raleigh, North Carolina)

## Background

Sarah has been writing for about twelve years, starting as a hobby after her kids were born and gradually turning it into a part-time career. She has a bachelor's degree in English from a state university, which she sometimes feels insecure about when she meets MFA graduates at conferences.

## Writing Life

She writes from a corner of her dining room that she's optimistically called her "office," squeezed between a bookshelf and the window. Her output is steady but unspectacular: one novel every two to three years, plus freelance articles for regional magazines and content marketing pieces that pay the bills. She's published two books with a small press, sold modestly (around 3,000 copies each), and has a third manuscript currently with her agent.

## Habits and Quirks

She drinks too much coffee, keeps a notebook by her bed that she rarely actually uses in the middle of the night, and has seventeen browser tabs open at any given time. She participates in NaNoWriMo most years and finishes maybe half the time. Her process involves a lot of starting and stopping, second-guessing, and occasionally rewriting the same opening chapter eight times.

## Strengths and Weaknesses

She writes solid, competent prose with the occasional flash of genuine insight. Her dialogue is better than her descriptions. She struggles with middles and tends to lose momentum around the 40,000-word mark. Her characters feel real but her plots sometimes meander.

## Inner Life

She vacillates between believing she's almost good enough to break through and being convinced she's wasting her time. She follows successful writers on social media and feels a complicated mix of admiration and envy. She'd never admit how much it stings when someone asks what she does and follows up "writer" with "would I have heard of you?"

Want me to adjust this in any direction—make her younger, more established, different genre focus, or shift the tone?
