# The Average Writer

**Name:** Dana Mercer

**Age:** 38

**Location:** A mid-sized city — somewhere with decent coffee shops but not a literary scene. Think Columbus, Ohio or Sacramento, California.

## Background

Dana has a bachelor's degree in English (or maybe communications — she switched majors twice). She's been "a writer" for about fifteen years, depending on how you count it. There was the year she freelanced full-time, the two years she ran a personal blog with modest traffic, and the ongoing novel that's been at 34,000 words for the better part of a decade.

Currently pays the bills with a content marketing job at a regional B2B software company. Writes blog posts about supply chain optimization. She's good at it, in the way that you become good at anything you do every weekday for six years.

## Work Habits

Writes most mornings before her day job, though "most" is doing a lot of work in that sentence. Has tried every productivity system — Scrivener, index cards, the bullet journal phase, that app that locks your phone in a virtual safe. Currently using Google Docs and willpower.

Her drafts are competent. Sentences land where they're supposed to. Paragraphs have topic sentences. The dialogue sounds like people talking, if those people were slightly more articulate than real people but less articulate than characters in good novels.

## What She's Reading

Whatever's on the bestseller table at Barnes & Noble, plus one literary novel a year that she feels she should read. Has strong opinions about books she hasn't finished. Listens to two writing podcasts and follows about forty authors on Bluesky.

## Aspirations and Anxieties

Wants to publish a novel. Worries that she started too late, that her voice isn't distinctive enough, that everyone on social media is more talented and better connected. Has 11,000 words of a short story collection she might pivot to. Has applied to three residencies and gotten into none.

Submits to literary magazines about twice a year. Has been published in two small journals you haven't heard of, which she's genuinely proud of and also slightly embarrassed by.

## The Thing That Makes Her Average

She's better than most people who say they want to write but don't. She's worse than the writers she compares herself to. She's somewhere in the wide middle where most actual writers live — the ones who keep going anyway, because not writing feels worse than writing does.
